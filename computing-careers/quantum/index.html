What Quantum Jobs Look Like in Industry Data

The government doesn't count quantum jobs yet. Industry surveys show a small, growing field with more engineering and business roles than research ones.

Question 1 of 3

What Jobs Exist in Quantum Computing?

Short answer: a few roles are deeply quantum-specific, but most jobs at quantum companies are engineering, software, and business roles that need mostly non-quantum skills.

A survey of 57 quantum companies sorted the jobs they hire for into two kinds.

Quantum-specific roles

  • Quantum algorithm developer
  • Error correction scientist

These need deep quantum knowledge and typically an advanced degree.

Broader roles at quantum companies

  • Software
  • Hardware
  • Business

Most of the skills these need aren't quantum-specific. Companies hire for them at the bachelor's, master's, and PhD levels.

How Big Is the Field, and Is It Growing?

Short answer: it's small but growing. Quantum-related openings worldwide have risen every year since 2021, and the U.S. has more quantum company workers and openings than any other country.

Job postings tell the same story. The share of U.S. job postings asking for quantum skills nearly tripled from 2011 to mid-2024. It peaked in 2019 and has stayed well above pre-2018 levels since (MIT Quantum Index Report 2025, using Lightcast data).

For scale: BLS expects about 95,300 software developer openings a year in the U.S. QED-C counted about 3,000 openings at U.S. quantum companies in 2025. That is why the established occupations on the main page remain the main path into quantum work.

How to read these numbers

QED-C's worker and U.S. openings counts cover only companies that work entirely on quantum technology, so quantum teams inside large tech companies aren't included. Its worldwide openings figure also includes quantum-specific openings at other organizations.

QED-C builds these estimates from company websites, job sites, and industry surveys. A company's workers are split across the countries where it operates, so country figures are estimates.

Question 3 of 3

Why Doesn't the Government Count Quantum Jobs?

Short answer: there are no federal codes yet for quantum industries, degree programs, or occupations, so researchers rely on keyword searches.

QED-C points to semiconductors for contrast. That industry has its own federal industry code, degree program code, and occupation code. Quantum technology has none of these, which is why counts like the ones above come from searching for "quantum" in company records and job postings.

The federal occupation system (SOC) is being revised for 2028, with the final structure expected in 2027. Whether it will add quantum occupations hasn't been announced. Until then, quantum work is counted inside established occupations such as computer and information research scientists and software developers, shown on the main page.

The Bottom Line

  • About 4,400 people worked at U.S. quantum companies in 2025, and openings worldwide have grown every year since 2021.
  • Engineering is the largest role at quantum companies. Researchers are under 10% of the workforce.
  • Outside the most specialized roles, quantum companies hire at the bachelor's, master's, and PhD levels.
  • Most roles at quantum companies need mostly non-quantum skills, so strong software and engineering skills matter as much as quantum coursework.
